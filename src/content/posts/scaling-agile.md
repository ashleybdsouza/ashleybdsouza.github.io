# TL;DR
>Problem: Agile broke down as we scaled from 10 → 100+ people
Role: Technical Program Manager leading org-wide transformation
Actions: Portfolio planning, dependency management, change leadership
Outcome: Faster delivery, higher morale, clearer ownership


We were a 10-person startup shipping fast around a single table.
At 100+ people across time zones, everything changed.

Sprints slipped. Dependencies exploded. Teams optimized locally and failed globally.

As the newly appointed TPM, I was tasked with one uncomfortable question:

How do you scale Agile without losing agility?

This post breaks down how I approached that transformation—what worked, what didn’t, and the metrics we used to prove progress.



### 1. My Role as TPM: From Sprint Support to Org-Level Orchestration
**My TPM Scope**
- **Org size**: ~100 people
- **Teams**: Multiple cross-functional squads
- **Problem space**: Delivery delays, dependency risk, siloed planning
- **Mandate**: Scale Agile without adding bureaucracy

**What changed in my role**
- From facilitating sprints -> To designing systems that aligned teams, leadership, and delivery

**How I operated**

- **Vision**: Connected Agile adoption to business outcomes

- **Strategy**: Built a phased roadmap (pilot → expand → standardize)

- **Change leadership**: Addressed resistance head-on, not passively

### 2. Turn “challenges” into decision records
**Challenge**
- Teams were prioritizing independently, causing missed dependencies and delayed releases.

**Decision**
- Adopted portfolio-level planning and Scrum of Scrums for cross-team visibility.

**Tradeoff**
- Increased coordination overhead, but significantly reduced surprise blockers.

**Result**
- Clear ownership of dependencies and earlier risk detection.

### 3. Metrics for Measuring Success
Metrics We Used to Prove the Transformation Worked
- Lead Time ↓ – features shipped faster end-to-end
- Deployment Frequency ↑ – smaller, safer releases
- Team Morale ↑ – survey scores improved quarter over quarter

### 4. What this post demostrates

- **TPM Skills**: Org-level planning, change management, metrics

- **Leadership**: Influencing without authority

- **Execution**: From ambiguity → operating model → outcomes

- **Systems Thinking**: Dependencies, flow, and feedback loops
